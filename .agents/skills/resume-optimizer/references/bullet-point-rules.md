# Bullet Point Rules — XYZ Format Guide

## The XYZ Formula

> **"Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]"**
> — Laszlo Bock, Former SVP of People Operations, Google

The formula forces three things at once:
- **X (What + Impact)**: Start with a strong action verb. State what you achieved, not what you were responsible for.
- **Y (Metric/Scale)**: Quantify the result. Numbers, percentages, dollar amounts, time saved, users reached, team size.
- **Z (Method/How)**: Briefly explain the approach that made the achievement possible.

Not every bullet needs all three in rigid order — vary sentence structure to avoid sounding robotic.
But every bullet must have **at minimum**: an action verb + one concrete detail + one measurable outcome or scale signal.

---

## Metric Inflation Rules

Metrics make bullets credible. When real metrics exist, use them. When they don't:

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| You know a rough range | Use the favorable end: "reduced time by 30–40%" → use "~35%" or "up to 40%" |
| Team-level metric you contributed to | Attribute clearly: "contributed to team hitting $2M ARR goal" |
| No metric but scale is known | Use scale signals: "across 12-person engineering team", "serving 5,000+ users", "for Fortune 500 client" |
| Completely unknown metric | Don't invent — use qualitative impact: "eliminated manual process", "became the team's go-to tool for X" |
| Time saved | Estimate from experience: if something took 4 hours and you cut it in half, say "saved ~2 hrs/week" |

**Rule**: If a number can be defended in an interview, it can go on the resume.
**Rule**: If you'd hesitate when asked "how did you arrive at that number?", remove it.

## Action Verb Tiers

Use **Tier 1** verbs for high-impact bullets. Rotate through verbs — never use the same verb twice in a section.

### Tier 1 — High Impact (use for top bullets)
Built, Launched, Drove, Led, Grew, Cut, Saved, Generated, Reduced, Scaled, Shipped, Delivered, Architected, Closed, Negotiated, Secured, Transformed, Revamped, Pioneered, Spearheaded (use sparingly — it's becoming a cliché)

### Tier 2 — Strong (use for supporting bullets)
Developed, Designed, Created, Implemented, Optimized, Improved, Automated, Migrated, Refactored, Deployed, Managed, Coordinated, Analyzed, Established, Streamlined, Consolidated, Onboarded, Trained, Presented, Authored

### Tier 3 — Use with caution (weak on their own)
Assisted, Supported, Helped, Contributed to, Participated in, Worked on, Handled, Maintained

> **If you're using a Tier 3 verb**, upgrade it by changing the subject: instead of "assisted with migration", say "co-led migration of legacy system to AWS, reducing infrastructure costs by 20%"

### Banned Action Verb Starters
- "Responsible for" (passive, describes a job, not an achievement)
- "Tasked with" (same problem)
- "Worked with" (too vague)
- "Helped" (diminishes ownership)
- "Was involved in" (vague non-commitment)

## Length & Formatting Rules

- **Length**: 1–2 lines max per bullet. Target 15–25 words. If it runs to 3 lines, split it or cut it.
- **Count per role**: Exactly 3 bullets for every experience and project entry. No exceptions.
  - Had more? Pick the 3 strongest and rewrite them.
  - Had fewer? Combine or split ideas to hit 3 strong bullets.
- **Tense**: Past tense for past roles. Present tense for current role only.
- **Punctuation**: No period at the end of bullets (standard resume convention)
- **Capitalization**: Only capitalize proper nouns and the first word of the bullet
- **Bullet symbol**: Use standard • (not ➤ ✓ → or other custom symbols — ATS unfriendly)
- **No sub-bullets**: Flat structure only for ATS compatibility
- **No summary section**: Never include or suggest an objective/summary at the top of a resume

## Bolding Rules

Bold exactly **one** element per bullet — the single thing a recruiter's eye should snap to when skimming.

**What to bold:**
- Numbers and percentages: `cut costs by **$80K/yr**`, `improved retention by **34%**`
- Hackathon/competition results: `won **2nd place** at HackHarvard`, `ranked **top 3** of 120 teams`
- Scale signals: `serving **50K DAU**`, `across **8-engineer** team`, `for **Fortune 500** client`
- Time saved / speed: `from **4 hours to 20 minutes**`, `shipped in **3 weeks**`

**What NOT to bold:**
- The action verb at the start of the bullet
- An entire phrase or clause
- Multiple things in one bullet (pick the single strongest)
- Generic words like "improved" or "built" without accompanying data

Good bolding:
- `Cut deployment time by **60%** by containerizing services with Docker`
- `Closed **$3.4M** in new ARR, finishing at 118% of quota`
- `Won **2nd place** at HackHarvard among 200+ teams with an IoT safety device`

Bad bolding:
- `**Cut deployment time by 60%** by containerizing services with Docker` (entire phrase — too much)
- `Cut deployment time by **60%** by containerizing services with **Docker**` (two bolded — pick one)
- No bolding at all (misses the visual anchor for fast-skimming recruiters)

The bold element is the thing that, if a recruiter sees only that word/number while scanning at 6-second pace,
they'd stop and read the whole bullet.

## Em Dash Rules

Em dashes (—) are a punctuation tool, not a stylistic default. Use them sparingly.

**Use an em dash when**: a sharp contrast or pivot genuinely reads better than a comma or period.
- Good: `Inherited a codebase with no tests — shipped 85% coverage in 10 weeks`
- (The dash here creates dramatic contrast that a comma would soften)

**Don't use an em dash when**: a comma, period, or "and" works perfectly fine.
- Bad: `Built the data pipeline — reduced processing time by 40%` (use "and" or a comma)
- Bad: `Led a team of 6 — delivered the project 2 weeks early` (period or comma is fine)

**Ceiling**: At most one em dash per role section. Most bullets should have zero.

## Structure Variations (to avoid robotic repetition)

The XYZ formula doesn't require the same word order every time. Valid variations:

1. **Impact-first**: "Cut deployment time by 60% by containerizing services with Docker"
2. **Method-first**: "Rebuilt the analytics dashboard in React, giving stakeholders real-time visibility into $4M in monthly spend"
3. **Scale-first**: "Across a team of 8 engineers, established code review standards that reduced bug rate by 25% over 2 quarters"
4. **Problem-solution**: "Inherited a system with 40% test coverage; brought it to 85% in 3 months by building automated CI/CD test suite"

Vary these across bullets within a role so the resume doesn't sound templated.

## Per-Industry Calibration

### Software / Engineering
- Lead with technical specificity: name the language, framework, tool
- Show scale: requests/sec, users, data volume, repo size, team size
- Include before/after: "from X to Y" is powerful
- Example: `"Refactored authentication service from monolith to microservices, reducing p99 latency from 800ms to 120ms"`

### Product Management
- Show ownership language: "owned", "defined", "drove"
- Tie to business outcomes: revenue, retention, activation, NPS
- Mention cross-functional coordination as a scale signal
- Example: `"Defined and shipped checkout redesign that increased conversion by 18% and drove $1.2M incremental ARR"`

### Marketing / Growth
- Channel specificity matters: "email", "paid social", "SEO", "content", not just "marketing"
- Metrics: CAC, ROAS, CTR, open rate, MQL volume, pipeline generated
- Example: `"Launched 8-email nurture sequence targeting mid-funnel leads, increasing MQL-to-SQL conversion by 22%"`

### Finance / Operations
- Process improvement bullets land well: time saved, error rate reduced, cost cut
- Compliance and risk bullets show maturity
- Example: `"Rebuilt monthly close process in Workday, reducing close cycle from 12 days to 6 and eliminating 3 manual reconciliation steps"`

### Sales
- Dollar amounts and quota attainment are king
- Deal size, sales cycle, account type give context
- Example: `"Closed $3.4M in new ARR in FY2023, finishing at 118% of quota by focusing on mid-market accounts in the healthcare vertical"`

## Quality Checklist (run before finalizing)

For each bullet, verify:
- [ ] Starts with a strong action verb (not "Responsible for", not a noun)
- [ ] Contains at least one number OR a concrete scale signal
- [ ] Specifies HOW the result was achieved (method/tool/approach)
- [ ] Is specific enough that it could NOT apply to someone else in the same job
- [ ] Does not use banned buzzwords (see anti-slop-rules.md)
- [ ] Is 1–2 lines (15–25 words ideally)
- [ ] Could be defended in an interview ("How did you get that number?")
- [ ] Doesn't start the same way as the bullet above or below it
- [ ] Has exactly 1 bolded element (the strongest metric or outcome)
- [ ] Uses an em dash only if a comma/period genuinely can't do the job

For each role/project section, verify:
- [ ] Exactly 3 bullets (not 2, not 4)
- [ ] At most 1 em dash across all 3 bullets
- [ ] No two bullets start with the same verb
