# Templates — Resume, Cover Letter, LinkedIn, Application Questions

Each template below defines the structure, field instructions, and population rules.
When filling a template, map candidate profile data to each field explicitly.
If a field cannot be filled from the profile, leave a [PLACEHOLDER — NEEDS INFO] marker.

---

## Template 1: Resume

### Structure
```
[FULL NAME]
[City, State] | [Phone] | [Email] | [LinkedIn URL] | [Portfolio/GitHub — if relevant]

SKILLS
[Grouped by category. List only skills mentioned in the JD or directly relevant.
Format: Category: Skill 1, Skill 2, Skill 3
E.g., Languages: Python, SQL, TypeScript | Tools: Tableau, dbt, Airflow | Cloud: AWS (S3, Lambda, RDS)]

EXPERIENCE

[Job Title — adjust for JD alignment if needed, flag adjustment]  
[Company Name] | [City, State or Remote] | [Month Year – Month Year or Present]

• [Bullet 1 — highest impact, most JD-relevant. Bold the top metric.]
• [Bullet 2 — second strongest achievement. Bold the top metric.]
• [Bullet 3 — technical depth or process/scale signal. Bold the top metric.]

[Repeat for each role — always exactly 3 bullets per role]

EDUCATION

[Degree, Major]
[University Name] | [Graduation Year]
[GPA if 3.5+, relevant honors, or relevant coursework — optional]

CERTIFICATIONS (optional section)
[Certification Name | Issuing Org | Year]

PROJECTS (optional — useful for early career or career changers)
[Project Name] | [Tech Stack] | [Month Year]
• [Bullet 1 — what you built and why. Bold the most impressive signal.]
• [Bullet 2 — your specific technical contribution. Bold the key metric or scale.]
• [Bullet 3 — outcome or impact. Bold the result.]
```

### Population Rules

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| Summary | **Do not include.** No summary, objective, or profile section. Resume starts with Skills. |
| Skills | Extract from JD's required/preferred skills. Cross-reference with candidate's actual skills. Only list what's real. |
| Job Titles | Can be adjusted to match JD's language. Flag all changes for candidate review. |
| Bullets | Exactly 3 per role and per project — no more, no fewer. XYZ format throughout. Bold 1 metric per bullet. |
| Dates | Month + Year format. Right-aligned (format in the actual doc). |
| Education | Include only if within 10 years or if GPA/honors is strong. Always include for recent grads. |

## Template 2: Cover Letter

### Structure
```
[Date]

Hiring Manager  
[Company Name]

Re: [Exact Job Title from Posting]

---

OPENING PARAGRAPH (3–5 sentences)
Hook: Don't start with "I am writing to express my interest". 
Instead: Open with a specific result, a connection to the company's work, or the core problem the role solves.
State who you are and why this role specifically.

BODY PARAGRAPH 1 — Theme: [JD Requirement #1]
Situation → Action → Result. Pick your strongest proof point for the top JD requirement.
2–4 sentences. One concrete example with a number if possible.

BODY PARAGRAPH 2 — Theme: [JD Requirement #2]
Same structure. Pick a different proof point. Can include a brief story/context.

BODY PARAGRAPH 3 — Theme: [JD Requirement #3 or Company Fit]
Why THIS company. Reference something specific: product, mission, recent news, team/values.
Then connect it to your background.

CLOSING (2–3 sentences)
Confident ask, not groveling. Reiterate your fit in one sentence.
Call to action: "I'd welcome the chance to talk through how my background in X fits the team's needs."
Do NOT use: "I look forward to discussing how I can contribute to your team's success."

[Your Name]
[Phone | Email | LinkedIn]
```

### Population Rules

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Opening hook | Choose from: strong metric ("I turned a 22-point NPS drop into a 15-point gain in 90 days"), specific draw ("I've been following [Company]'s work on X since..."), or role-specific insight ("The challenge of [problem this role solves] is one I've navigated at [Company]...") |
| Body paragraphs | Map to JD's top 3 requirements. Each paragraph = one proof point from the candidate's history. |
| Company connection | Must be specific. Generic "I admire your commitment to innovation" is slop. Name a product, customer story, funding round, mission statement, or team initiative. |
| Length | 3–5 paragraphs. Max 400 words. Recruiters will not read more. |
| Tone | Match the company's tone (startup = direct/casual, enterprise = professional but not stiff) |

## Template 3: LinkedIn Profile

### Headline (120 characters max)
```
[Role/Title] | [Value prop or domain] | [Signal: company, achievement, or who you help]
```
Examples:
- `Product Manager | B2B SaaS | Ex-Stripe | Scaled checkout products to $50M ARR`
- `Data Engineer | Building real-time pipelines | AWS Certified | Open to new roles`
- `UX Designer | Making complex products feel simple | Healthcare & Fintech`

### About Section (300–500 words)
```
[Opening: 1–2 sentences in first person — who you are and what you care about professionally]

[Body: 2–3 short paragraphs covering:
 - What you do / your domain expertise
 - A highlight or career proof point (with metric)
 - What kind of problems/companies/teams you thrive in]

[Closing: What you're looking for or open to — optional but useful if job seeking]

[Skills/tools callout: Optional one-liner like "Tools I use daily: Python, Spark, dbt, Snowflake, Airflow"]
```

### Experience Bullets (LinkedIn)
- Same XYZ format as resume
- Can be slightly more conversational (LinkedIn is a semi-social platform)
- 3–5 bullets per role, most recent role gets most space
- Include media/links/projects in the LinkedIn-native fields if available

### Population Rules

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| Headline | Rewrite per target role. Should mirror the JD's job title language. |
| About | First person. Specific. Should match the resume's story but can be warmer in tone. |
| Skills section | Prioritize skills listed in the JD. LinkedIn's algorithm surfaces profiles that match skills to job postings. |
| Connections | Remind candidate: having 500+ connections increases discoverability. |

## Template 4: Application Questions

Common formats and how to answer them:

### "Tell us about yourself" / "Walk me through your background"
Structure: Present → Past → Future
```
I'm currently [role/what you do] at [company/context], where I focus on [domain].
Before that, I [key past experience with one proof point].
I'm drawn to [this company/role] because [specific reason tied to JD].
```
Keep to 90 seconds if spoken, 3–4 sentences if written.

### Behavioral Questions (STAR format)
"Tell me about a time when..." / "Describe a situation where..."
```
Situation: [Brief context — 1 sentence. Don't over-explain.]
Task: [What was your specific role/responsibility? 1 sentence.]
Action: [What did YOU do? Be specific. 2–4 sentences. Use "I", not "we".]
Result: [Quantified outcome. What changed? 1–2 sentences.]
```
Max 200–250 words written. For video/spoken: 90–120 seconds.

### "Why this company?"
```
Structure:
1. Something specific you admire (product, mission, recent news — not "culture of innovation")
2. How it connects to your background/values
3. What you want to contribute
```
Avoid: "I've always admired your company" / "The opportunity for growth"

### "Why are you leaving / why did you leave?"
Keep it forward-looking and neutral. Never badmouth a previous employer.
```
Template: "I [achieved X / learned Y] at [Company], and I'm looking for [something specific this role offers].
The [specific aspect of this role] is a direct match for the direction I want to grow."
```

### "What's your greatest weakness?"
Pick a real weakness that:
- Is not a core competency of the role
- Shows self-awareness
- Shows you're actively working on it
```
Template: "I tend to [genuine weakness]. I've noticed this [specific example of when it showed up].
I've been working on it by [concrete step you're taking], and I've seen improvement in [how]."
```

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
Connect your answer to skills you'll build in this role. Don't say you want their boss's job.
```
Template: "I want to deepen my expertise in [domain relevant to role] and grow into [adjacent skill or scope].
This role seems like an ideal path to get there because [specific aspect]."
```

## Filling Instructions for AI

When filling any template:

1. **Identify all available data** from the candidate profile first
2. **Map to template fields** — be explicit about which profile data fills which field
3. **Flag gaps** with [PLACEHOLDER — NEEDS: description of what's missing]
4. **Never invent data** to fill a template field — either derive it from existing profile info or flag it
5. **Adjust tone** per template (resume = formal, cover letter = professional-warm, LinkedIn = semi-casual, application Q = conversational)
6. **Return a complete filled template** with all placeholders clearly marked so the candidate knows exactly what to provide
